## 1. `/tips/linen-closet-organization`

- **Title tag:** Linen closet organization: labels and sheet sets | Home Harmonics
- **Meta description:** A simple system for a linen closet that stays organized: label what you can't see, store sheets as a set, and edit before you buy bins.
- **Image:** `05-label-what-you-cant-see.jpg`, `07-store-sheets-as-a-set.jpg`
- **H1:** Linen closet organization that stays organized

**Intro:** A linen closet fails for two reasons: you can't see what's inside, and nothing stays together. Two habits fix most of it.

**## Label what you can't see**
Opaque bins and baskets need a label. Clear bins need an edit instead, because everyone can see what's inside, and a crowded clear bin looks as messy as it is. Keep labels short and use the same words every time: Towels, Blankets, Pillows, Cleaning Supplies.

**## Store sheets as a set**
Fold the flat sheet, fitted sheet and pillowcases together, then tuck the whole set inside one of its own pillowcases. When you open the shelf, you find the whole bed in one piece. Group sets by bed size (King, Queen, Full, Twin) and add a small label on the front.

**## Edit first**
Before buying anything, take everything out. Keep two sets of sheets per bed and the towels you actually use. Donate the rest. A smaller edit means smaller, calmer storage.

**## How we handle it**
At Home Harmonics, we measure the shelves first, edit with you, and only then choose baskets and labels, so the storage fits the linen and not the other way around.

## 2. `/tips/garage-storage-ideas`

- **Title tag:** Garage storage ideas: go vertical | Home Harmonics, Dallas–Fort Worth
- **Meta description:** Wall systems for tools and bikes, overhead racks for seasonal bins, and a clear floor. Plus a real garage project from Dallas–Fort Worth.
- **Image:** `06-go-vertical.jpg`, `02-garage-fully-fitted.jpg`
- **H1:** Garage storage ideas: use the walls and ceiling

**Intro:** The floor is the most valuable space in a garage, and it's usually the first thing to fill up. The fix is to move almost everything up.

**## Walls for what you use often**
A wall system holds bikes, tools, helmets and garden equipment off the floor. Put the things you reach for weekly at arm's height, and the occasional items higher.

**## Overhead racks for seasonal bins**
Holiday decorations, camping gear and off-season items belong overhead. Use matching, labeled bins so you can see what's in each from below.

**## Cabinets for the small stuff**
Closed cabinets hide the clutter that makes a garage feel chaotic: paint, cleaning supplies, small tools. Group by category, one shelf per category.

**## Keep the floor clear**
If it isn't a car, a mat or a workbench, it shouldn't live on the floor. A clear floor is what makes the space feel finished, and it's easier to clean.

**## A real project**
In one Dallas–Fort Worth garage, bins, bikes and boxes covered the floor. The finished garage has wall storage, overhead racks and cabinets, and room for the car again. Planned by Yaz. Built by a partner. Organized in two days.

**## How we handle it**
We plan the layout, coordinate the build with a trusted partner, and then organize everything into its place, so the garage is ready to use when we leave.

## 3. `/tips/move-in-checklist`

- **Title tag:** Move-in checklist: what to unpack first | Home Harmonics
- **Meta description:** Unpack in this order: beds and bath, kitchen essentials, then everything else. A calm checklist for your first 48 hours in a new home.
- **Image:** `04-unpack-in-this-order.jpg`
- **H1:** Move-in checklist: what to unpack first

**Intro:** The first night in a new home should feel like home. Unpack in a deliberate order and the rest can wait a week.

**## Unpack in this order**
1. **Beds and bath.** Make the beds, set out towels and toiletries. You'll be tired, and this is what you need first.
2. **Kitchen essentials.** Plates, cups, a pan, coffee, basic cutlery, and a few staples.
3. **Everything else.** Room by room, starting with the spaces you use most.

**## Your first 48 hours**
- Label boxes by room on the way in, not after.
- Break down boxes as you empty them so the floor stays clear.
- Put things where you'll use them, not where they fit. You can refine later.
- Leave decor and the least-used rooms for last.

**## Moving to Dallas–Fort Worth?**
Settling in is easier with a plan. If you're relocating, decide before you arrive who will set up the kitchen, closets and bedrooms, so the first week isn't spent in boxes.

**## How we handle it**
Home Harmonics leads move-in setup from the first walkthrough to the final handover, with one accountable lead throughout.

**CTA:** shared block. **Related:** closet organization, kids' closet organization.

## 4. `/tips/closet-organization`

- **Title tag:** Closet organization: edit, one in one out, swap twice a year | Home Harmonics
- **Meta description:** A closet only stays edited if something leaves when something arrives. Three simple rules for a closet that works: edit first, one in one out, and swap seasons twice a year.
- **Image:** `11-one-in-one-out.jpg`, `12-swap-twice-a-year.jpg`
- **H1:** Closet organization: three rules that keep it edited

**Intro:** A beautiful closet is mostly an edited one. These rules keep it that way.

**## 1. Edit first**
Take everything out. Keep what you wear, fits and feels like you. Move the rest out before you buy a single bin or hanger.

**## 2. One in, one out**
A closet only stays edited if something leaves when something arrives. When you bring in a new piece, choose one to donate or sell.

**## 3. Swap twice a year**
Off-season clothes go high and back. Everyday clothes go at arm's reach. Doing this twice a year takes under an afternoon and keeps the closet fitted to the season.

**## Zones, not piles**
Group by category (tops, trousers, dresses, outerwear), then by color within each. A closet you can read at a glance is a closet you'll keep tidy.

**## How we handle it**
We edit with you, never for you, and then set up the closet so it's easy to maintain.

**CTA:** shared block. **Related:** how to store sweaters, drawer organization.

## 5. `/tips/how-to-store-sweaters`

- **Title tag:** How to store sweaters: fold, don't hang | Home Harmonics
- **Meta description:** Hanging stretches the shoulders. Fold knits to the width of the shelf. A simple guide to storing sweaters so they keep their shape.
- **Image:** `08-knits-fold-never-hang.jpg`
- **H1:** How to store sweaters: fold, don't hang

**Intro:** Knits are the exception to "hang everything." Hanging a sweater stretches the shoulders over time.

**## How to fold**
1. Lay the sweater flat, front down.
2. Fold each sleeve across the back.
3. Fold the sweater in half, or in thirds, to the width of your shelf.
4. Stack neatly, heaviest and darkest at the bottom.

**## Shelf tips**
- Fold to the width of the shelf so stacks sit flush.
- Keep stacks to a few sweaters so you can see and reach what you need.
- Use a shelf, not a bin, for everyday knits.

**## How we handle it**
We set up shelving for knits so everything is visible, uncrushed and easy to put back.

## 6. `/tips/drawer-organization`

- **Title tag:** Drawer organization: one compartment, one category | Home Harmonics
- **Meta description:** Size dividers to what lives inside. If it slides, it isn't divided. A simple guide to organizing drawers for watches, wallets, sunglasses and small items.
- **Image:** `09-one-compartment-one-category.jpg`
- **H1:** Drawer organization: one compartment, one category

**Intro:** A drawer works when everything has a home. The goal is one compartment for one category.

**## Start with what lives inside**
Empty the drawer, sort by category, and measure the biggest items first. Choose dividers to fit the items, not the other way around.

**## Rule of thumb**
If it slides, it isn't divided. Items shouldn't move around when you open and close the drawer. Snug compartments keep watches, wallets, sunglasses and small items in place.

**## Keep it visible**
Use shallow, open compartments so you can see everything from above. Put what you use daily at the front.

**## How we handle it**
We plan drawers around what you own, so the dividers fit exactly and stay put.

**CTA:** shared block. **Related:** closet organization.

## 7. `/tips/kids-closet-organization`

- **Title tag:** Kids closet organization: store at their height | Home Harmonics
- **Meta description:** Keep everyday items within a child's reach and rotate toys instead of stacking them. Simple ideas for kids' closets and rooms.
- **Image:** `10-organize-at-their-height.jpg`
- **H1:** Kids closet organization: store at their height

**Intro:** Kids keep a space tidy when they can reach it. Organize at their height.

**## Everyday items within reach**
Clothes they wear daily, favorite books and a few toys go on low shelves or open bins. Put out-of-season and special items higher.

**## Rotate, don't stack**
Keep only a few toys out at a time and store the rest. Rotate every few weeks so old toys feel new. Fewer things out means easier cleanup.

**## Make it easy to put away**
Open bins with handles beat lids. One bin per category: blocks, books, stuffed animals.

**## How we handle it**
We design kids' spaces around how each child moves, so they can take part in keeping it tidy.

**CTA:** shared block. **Related:** closet organization, declutter before you buy storage.

## 8. `/tips/declutter-before-you-buy-storage`

- **Title tag:** Declutter before you buy storage: edit first, then buy | Home Harmonics
- **Meta description:** Measure the space, keep only what you use, then choose storage last. Why baskets and bins can't fix an overfull shelf.
- **Image:** `03-edit-first-then-buy.jpg`
- **H1:** Edit first. Then buy.

**Intro:** The most common organizing mistake is buying storage first. Baskets can't fix an overfull shelf.

**## 1. Measure the space**
Know the exact width, depth and height before you shop. It prevents returns and wasted space.

**## 2. Keep only what you use**
Take everything out, sort into keep, donate and discard, and be honest about what you actually use.

**## 3. Choose storage last**
Once you know what's staying, pick storage to fit it. You'll buy less, and it will fit.

**## How we handle it**
Our process is edit first, then design and set up the storage. Nothing is bought until we know what it needs to hold.

**CTA:** shared block. **Related:** closet organization, garage storage ideas.
