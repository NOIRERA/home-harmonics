## What sets the length of a project

Five things decide how long whole-home organization takes:

1. **The number of spaces in scope.** A kitchen, a pantry and two closets is a different project from every room in the house.
2. **How much needs editing.** Deciding what stays, what goes and what is donated is usually the slowest part, and the most valuable.
3. **How quickly decisions are made.** Some clients want to be part of every choice. Others prefer to agree categories in advance and let the work move.
4. **Products.** Inserts, canisters and hangers have to be measured, ordered and delivered. Sourcing in advance keeps the days on site for installing.
5. **The household's calendar.** Work goes fastest when the spaces in scope are free to work in.

## Why consecutive days matter

Organization done in scattered sessions tends to stall. A room is half finished, life fills it back up, and the next session starts by redoing the last one. Scheduling whole-home work as consecutive days keeps momentum, keeps the house in a state of change for as short a time as possible, and means every room ends finished rather than "in progress." It is why Home Harmonics books engagements in blocks, not an hour here and there.

## What the days look like

Every project is different, but the shape is consistent:

- **Before the first day:** a walkthrough, a household profile and a design plan you approve, with products ordered to fit.
- **The first days:** editing in the spaces with the most in them, with donations and removals cleared as the work goes.
- **The later days:** installing products, putting everything in its place and labeling.
- **The last hour:** a walkthrough of every space and a care guide written for your household.

## How to make it faster

- **Decide categories in advance.** Agreeing, for example, that unworn clothing goes to donation saves dozens of small decisions.
- **Approve the design plan quickly**, so products can be ordered early.
- **Keep the days clear.** Fewer interruptions means more finished rooms.
- **Plan for what leaves.** Donation and removal are part of the work, so the editing never stalls on where things should go.
