## Why change the wardrobe over at all

A closet that holds every season at once is a closet where the things you wear are hidden among the things you don't. Moving the off-season to storage frees the best space, the hanging and shelving between waist and eye height, for what you actually reach for this month. It is also the natural moment to edit, because every piece passes through your hands.

## When to do it

In North Texas the seasons are uneven, so the date matters less than the weather. Two changeovers a year usually work: one in spring, once the cool mornings have passed, and one in autumn, when the first cold front settles in. Keep a small transition edit within reach, such as a light jacket and a few long sleeves, for the weeks when the weather changes its mind.

## Edit as you go

As each piece comes off the rail, ask three questions:

1. **Did I wear it this season?** If not, decide whether it stays for a reason or goes.
2. **Does it fit and is it in good repair?** Set aside anything that needs tailoring, cleaning or mending, and book it in before it is stored.
3. **Would I buy it again?** If not, it is a candidate for donation or consignment.

Keep the decisions moving. The aim is a wardrobe that works, not a perfect one.

## Clean before storing

Store clothing only once it is clean. Body oils and invisible stains set over months and attract moths. Launder or dry-clean everything going into storage, and let it dry completely.

## Store it so it comes back as it left

- **Use breathable containers.** Fabric boxes or garment bags protect clothing without trapping moisture. Avoid sealing natural fibers in plastic for long periods.
- **Fold knitwear; hang structured pieces.** Sweaters stretch on hangers. Tailoring and coats keep their shape on proper hangers inside garment bags.
- **Keep it cool and dry.** A closet shelf, an under-bed drawer or a climate-controlled storage room is better than a hot attic or garage.
- **Protect against moths** with cedar or lavender, refreshed each season.

## Label everything

Label each container with the season and the category ("Autumn: knitwear", "Spring: linen trousers"), on the end that faces out. The next changeover then takes a fraction of the time, and anyone helping in the house can find what is asked for.

## Reset the closet zones

With the new season in place, put what you wear most at eye level and closest to the door, group by category and then by color, and keep shoes and accessories for the season within reach. A closet set up this way tends to stay that way, because putting things back is as easy as taking them out.
